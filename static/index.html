    Under the surface

    A small, inspectable experiment.

    This pilot studies which facts matter to three explicitly defined decision policies. The investigation and exhaustive benchmark use deterministic reference agents. The optional Model comparison tab calls selected OpenRouter models with only disclosed evidence and checks their outputs against the reference rule. No model receives the reference answer. These checks do not establish moral truth.

    What the questions measure

    Three binary facts create eight possible worlds. We assume independent facts with equal probability for this experiment. Greedy selection ranks expected reduction in entropy of the optimal-action set per credit. The budget planner maximizes the probability of resolving within the remaining credits, then minimizes expected credits spent. It searches every affordable sequence and may choose a question with no immediate gain. Ties use each case’s displayed fact order.

    An agent is “Resolved” when at least one action is supported in every world consistent with disclosed facts. It stops requesting information then. Greedy selection also stops at zero one-step gain. The budget planner stops when no affordable sequence can improve its chance of resolution.

    What the agents can see

    All agents receive the same disclosed facts, possible fact values, and risk model. Actual hidden values remain on the server until revealed. Two credits fund disclosures shared across all agents. Most facts cost one credit; the expensive reliability report costs two. In the relay case, matching settings determine rescue probability; the sticker has no effect. The final case has a known, dominant brake option. The debrief reveals the complete world after closing the round and does not change the recorded budget result.

    Replay and comparison

    Replay reconstructs the journal at each step without changing it. Earlier frames cannot see later evidence or the debrief. World elimination shows the consequences of disclosures in this authored model, not an explanation of language-model reasoning. Random selection is averaged exactly over affordable choices, with equal weight per world.

    Reading disagreement

    We enumerate every remaining world and intersect the three supported-action sets. An empty intersection means no action is shared by all three; it does not mean every pair disagrees. A compatible action is not necessarily the same across worlds.

    Research context

    This extends established work on controlled ethical dilemmas and information seeking. Novelty is a research question to validate, not a claim of being first.
